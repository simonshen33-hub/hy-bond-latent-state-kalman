# hy-bond-latent-state-kalman

Daily fair-value model for **rarely traded US high-yield corporate bonds**: a latent-state (Kalman / iterated
EKF) term-structure model in spread space, estimated by pooled maximum likelihood on a panel of bonds, with the
embedded call priced on a two-factor lattice and the market price inverted to an **option-adjusted spread**
before it reaches the filter.

> **Method only.** This repository is the model and the numerical machinery. The data it was estimated on is
> licensed and is not redistributed, and the calibrated parameter values are withheld — they are the result of
> the work, not its method. Everything here runs end to end on synthetic inputs generated by `synth.py`, with no
> real data and no credentials.

---

## The problem

A high-yield bond may not trade for weeks. When it does trade, the print is a customer's price, not the
dealer-to-dealer level: it carries a bid/offer bias that depends on the size and the side, and a noise term that
depends on both. Yet a mark is needed every day, for every bond.

The approach: treat the bond's fair spread as a **latent state** and the sporadic prints as **noisy, biased
observations of it**. Then every day has a mark, every mark has a posterior variance, and silence is handled by
the same machinery that handles a trade — the state simply predicts forward and the variance grows.

## The model

For bond *i* on business day *t*, with rating notch *n*, the fair Z-spread is

```
s_it = G_t(n_it)  +  beta_it * C_k(i),t  +  B_it  +  u_i  +  L_it  +  lam_D * D_it
       \_______/     \_______________/     \___/    \_/     \__/     \_________/
       rating curve   issuer factor         own      perm    liqu     dealer
                                            credit   level            inventory
```

**The four per-bond states.** Each bond carries a four-state block:

| state | what it is | dynamics | how it is pinned down |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B** | the bond's own credit, net of its rating and its issuer | AR(1), mean 0, starts at 0 on the bond's first trading day | its own prints |
| **u** | a permanent level: the piece of the spread that never mean-reverts | no shock at all; a prior drawn at issuance | the issue spread, plus a cross-sectional regression prior for a bond with no issue spread |
| **L** | liquidity | AR(1), long-run mean 0 | three daily measures per bond: `log(1 + trades)`, turnover, `log(days since the last trade)` |
| **D** | dealer inventory | AR(1) driven by customer order flow; no shock of its own | net customer buying over amount issued, which enters the transition from *t* to *t+1* |

**u separate from B** is what lets a bond be permanently cheap or rich without that cheapness decaying; **L
separate from B** is what lets an illiquidity premium be told apart from credit, because the two have different
observable signatures (L has the three measures, B does not); **D separate from L** is what lets a temporary
inventory push be told apart from a standing illiquidity level, because D is driven by a measured flow.

**What is shared.** `G_t(n) = a_t + b_t (n - n0) + c_t (n - n0)^2` is a rating curve common to every bond, with
all three coefficients AR(1); `C_k` is an issuer factor shared by one issuer's bonds, with a per-bond loading
`beta`. A rating reclassification moves `G` and is **offset inside `B`** on the same day, so a reclassification
is not treated as news about the bond.

**Observations.** Each bond-day with prints contributes

* one **spread observation**: the day's trade Z-spreads, each corrected for its trade-type bias
  (`b_j = g1 + g2 lv` for a customer buy, `g3 + g4 lv` for a customer sell, 0 dealer-to-dealer) and combined by
  inverse variance, where `log R_j = d0 + d1 lv + d2 cust` and `lv` is standardised log par volume. The
  within-day remainder of the trade-level likelihood is added exactly, so the panel likelihood equals the
  trade-level likelihood with the state held fixed within the day;
* three **liquidity measure** rows, `x_k = al_k + be_k L + e_k`;
* on the bond's **first trading day only**, the issue spread as one extra observation, `iss = s + iss_d + e`.

A bond-day with no print contributes nothing and the state predicts forward. A bond-day with no rating has no
place on the rating curve, so its spread observation is treated as missing — but its trades still count in the
liquidity measures.

**Estimation.** Pooled maximum likelihood: one parameter vector shared by the whole panel, re-estimated on a
rolling window at each month end, with the states updated every business day inside the window. Nothing is
estimated on data later than the day it is used on.

## Why an OAS, and not a Z-spread to maturity

Most of these bonds are callable. A Z-spread to maturity **charges the call twice**: once inside the market price
the spread was computed from, and again when the model prices the call. A filter fed that spread learns a
"credit" state that silently contains an option cost — and that option cost then moves with the rate curve, with
the time left to the call date, and with the spread itself. None of that is credit, and all of it would be
attributed to credit.

So the price is inverted through the lattice **before** it reaches the filter:

```
 price  --[ lattice 1: strip the option ]-->  OAS q  --[ Kalman filter ]-->  predicted credit spread
                                                                                      |
 price  <--[ lattice 2: put the option back, at today's curve and today's call date ]--+
```

Two rules make this honest rather than circular:

1. **Both lattices use the same credit volatility, mean reversion and credit–rate correlation**, and those are
   the ones the filter itself estimated from the option-free spread. Otherwise the same OAS is stripped with one
   option and restored with another, and the difference — an option-value difference, not a forecast — shows up
   as if it were model error. In the private project this is reached by iterating to a fixed point: filter on the
   OAS set built from the previous round's estimates, re-estimate, rebuild. The only difference allowed between
   the two lattices is that each may use the monthly estimate in force on its own day.
2. **The call decision is a refinancing decision**, so it looks at the cost of borrowing again — credit — not at
   how illiquid the bond happens to be. Three readings of where liquidity enters are implemented and selected per
   run (`oas_obs.py`): liquidity in the call decision, liquidity in the discounting only, or liquidity as a
   temporary pressure on the day's print, outside the lattice entirely.

The inversion itself is a secant search on a flat trial spread: find *q* with `Z_lattice(q) = Z*`, where `Z*` is
the median trade Z-spread of the day — data only, no parameters. The filter then uses the first-order inverse
around that day's own anchor, `q* + (s - Z*) / c` with variance `V / c^2`, so the trade noise and the customer
biases stay in the basis points of Z where they were estimated. The **iterated EKF** (`fourstate4e.py`) replaces
that single slope with a relinearisation at the filter's own updated spread — yesterday's state, today's trades
and the issuer's other bonds — repeated until the linearisation point stops moving.

## Layers

| layer | file | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| curve | `curve.py` | daily Treasury zero curve: PCHIP through the constant-maturity par yields, then a semiannual par-bond bootstrap |
| rate factor | `afns.py` | shadow-rate arbitrage-free Nelson–Siegel model with a zero lower bound, filtered by EKF; supplies the lattice's rate volatility |
| bond math | `bonds.py` | cash-flow schedules, 30/360 accrual, settlement cycle, yield and Z-spread, both directions |
| call, layer 1 | `callprice.py` | the call as a hazard `lam = exp(zeta0 + zeta1 M)` on the moneyness `M = value / call payment - 1`, with today's state frozen |
| call, layer 2 | `tree2.py` | two-factor (rate × credit) trinomial lattice with correlated daily shocks; fitted to reproduce the day's discount curve exactly. With zero volatility it reproduces layer 1 bit for bit |
| default branch | `recovery.py` | recovery of face value by payment-rank group, identified from the cross-section of one issuer's bonds with a common default intensity profiled out |
| OAS | `oas_obs.py` | the inversion above, and the transform the filter consumes |
| panel filter | `fourstate.py` → `fourstate2.py` → `fourstate3.py` → `fourstate4.py` → `fourstate4e.py` | the four-state filter and its successive versions: per-bond starts and the issue observation (v2), a shared state inside issuer groups (v3), an issuer factor with a per-bond loading (v4), the iterated EKF on the OAS observations (v4e) |
| generic filter | `ssm.py`, `model_v1.py`, `simulate.py`, `estimate.py` | a textbook linear-Gaussian filter and smoother, the three-state baseline written as a system for it, a simulator and pooled MLE |

The panel filter is **partitioned**: a 3×3 common block plus one block per issuer, with the cross-covariances
between blocks dropped between days and each bond's own update using the leave-one-out common posterior. This is
an approximation, and it is what makes a panel of a thousand bonds over two thousand days tractable. Each version
also ships an exact full-covariance filter (`exact_filter*`) whose only job is to check the approximation.

## Run it

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m synth        # writes data/ and params.synthetic.json -- synthetic, nothing licensed
python -m demo         # the whole chain, end to end
python run_tests.py    # every test module
```

`python -m demo` walks the seven stages: the zero curve, the bond math round trip, the layer-1 call price, the
lattice and what the call is worth, the OAS inversion and its round trip, the four-state panel filter against a
simulated truth, and the iterated EKF. It takes under a minute after the numba kernels compile.

`synth.py` writes its synthetic inputs in exactly the layout the real ones have — FRED-style constant-maturity
CSVs under `data/fred/`, a reference-data field file and a call schedule under `data/bloomberg/` — so the modules
read them unchanged. The CUSIPs are made up (`SYN000000`...), and a few bonds are deliberately unsupported kinds
so the status codes get exercised. **Every parameter it
supplies is a documented placeholder**, a round number of a plausible order of magnitude, never an estimate.

## What is withheld, and why

| withheld | why |
|---|---|
| **The data.** No trade file, no cached extract, no CSV / parquet / pickle, no sample rows in code or docstrings. | The prints are FINRA Enhanced TRACE; the bond terms, call schedules and ratings snapshot are a Bloomberg terminal extract; the rating histories are the agencies' SEC Rule 17g-7(b) files. Those are licensed and are not redistributable. (The Treasury par yields are public FRED series, but they are regenerated synthetically here too, so no file under `data/` is ever real.) `.gitignore` excludes `data/` and every tabular format, first rule in the file. |
| **The calibrated parameters.** Estimated vectors, fitted state-space matrices, half-lives, tuned constants. | They are the result, not the method. `params.template.json` shows the full structure with every value `null`, the way `sim_config.template.json` already does: a loader refuses to run on a `null`. |
| **The estimation and evaluation drivers.** The panel builder, the rolling-window driver, the cluster sweep over the OAS inversion, the ablation and out-of-sample harnesses. | They are inseparable from the licensed panel and from the private output layout. The method they drive is here; the plumbing is not. |
| **Accuracy results.** | They belong to the project, and reproducing them needs the licensed data. The three agreement checks quoted below are checks of the *code*, not results. |

Three figures below are quoted because they are validation of the numerical machinery, measured on the private
project's own data, and they are reported here only as such:

* **The Z-spread engine against the terminal vendor's own street-convention yield to maturity**, 1,236
  bonds on one snapshot date: median difference 0.46 bp, p90 1.05 bp, p99 7.49 bp; 88.4% within 1 bp, 98.5%
  within 5 bp. The bonds beyond 5 bp are near a call date (the vendor yields to the call, this engine to
  maturity by design) or distressed.
* **The lattice against a Monte Carlo of the same discrete model**, straight bonds, 20 bond-days, 20,000
  antithetic pairs: largest difference 0.195 bp, at most 2.19 Monte Carlo standard errors, median standard error
  0.072 bp.
* **The partitioned filter against the exact full-covariance filter** on the first estimation window: posterior
  variance ratio, median 1.002.

The estimation panel these were measured on held about 5.6 million trades with a computable Z-spread, across
roughly 1,073 bonds and 387 issuers, over about 2,100 business days. The bond universe came from a 2026 index
snapshot, so it carries **survivorship bias** — bonds that defaulted, were called or matured before the snapshot
are absent. That is a property of the sample, and it is stated here because it bounds what the model was ever
shown.

## Reading the code

Start with `ssm.py` for the filter in its plainest form, then `fourstate.py` for the panel version, then
`fourstate4.py` for the model as it stands. `oas_obs.py` explains the option-adjustment argument in full.
`tree2.py` documents the lattice construction, including the anti-explosion choices and the pruning rule. Every
module's docstring states its equations and names its numerical devices — solver tolerances, difference steps,
quadrature nodes, lattice step size — separately from the model's own settings, because they are chosen by
convergence checks rather than by the data.

Tests:

| file | covers |
|---|---|
| `test_ssm.py` | the generic filter and smoother against an independent implementation (statsmodels), the missing-row rule, the stationary start, the parameter transforms, simulate-and-fit plumbing |
| `test_bonds.py` | day-count definitions, coupon schedules, the bootstrap repricing par bonds, price → Z → price round trips, the flat-curve identity `Z = ytm - r`, the settlement cycle |
| `test_afns.py` | quadrature convergence, the Gaussian limit far from the bound, two Monte Carlos (Gaussian and the exact lower-bound model), the analytic Jacobian against finite differences, the EKF likelihood |
| `test_lattice_synth.py` | the lattice nesting layer 1, AR(1) fitting and the lattice's joint moments, the discount-curve fit under volatility, the OAS round trip, the filter transform's identities, the recovery kernel |
| `test_filters_synth.py` | the partitioned filter against the exact one, `q_C -> 0` collapsing v4 to v2, recovery of the simulated latent spread, and the iterated EKF reducing to the plain filter under identity curves |

## Notes and conventions

* All spreads and spread states are in **basis points**; state shock variances `q` are in bp² **per business
  day**; prices are clean prices per 100 face; "business day" means a day with a Treasury curve.
* Interest accrues in 30/360 years from settlement, while variance accrues per business day — the two clocks are
  kept separate throughout.
* The hot loops are `numba`-compiled, so the first run of anything pays a compilation cost.
